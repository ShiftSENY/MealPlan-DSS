Why a meal planning DSS?

The idea behind the system

Among the different decision support system ideas, weekly meal planning addresses a problem that I encounter almost every day. Deciding what to cook or eat may seem like a simple task, but it requires repeatedly considering available ingredients, nutritional balance, cooking time, budget, and personal preferences. Because this decision occurs several times a day, it gradually consumes a significant amount of mental energy. At the same time, I need to focus on my studies and other personal responsibilities. Continuously thinking about my next meal occupies mental space that could be devoted to my academics and other important matters.

My desired DSS will assist me in deciding what meals to prepare throughout the week by evaluating multiple decision criteria. The system will consider factors such as available ingredients, nutritional requirements, budget, cooking time, ingredient expiration dates, meal variety, and dietary preferences. Using these inputs, it will rank and recommend the most suitable meal options for each day or meal period while explaining why each recommendation is appropriate.

Unlike many decisions that occur occasionally, meal planning is repetitive and unavoidable. Reducing the cognitive effort required for this daily decision can improve my productivity, lessen decision fatigue, and make it easier to maintain healthy eating habits.

How it works

From groceries to a full week plan in five steps

  1. 1

    Tell it what you have

    Log the ingredients in your kitchen — or import a shopping list. Expiry dates can be added in seconds.

  2. 2

    Set your constraints

    Pick a budget, a daily cooking time limit, dietary preferences and nutritional targets.

  3. 3

    Run the evaluation

    The system scores every viable meal candidate against all of your criteria at once.

  4. 4

    Get ranked recommendations

    Each day and meal period gets a ranked shortlist — best match first.

  5. 5

    Understand the “why”

    Every recommendation is explained: which criterion it scored well on, and which you traded off.

Key features

What the system weighs before it recommends

Ingredient tracking

The plan is built from what is actually in your kitchen, not from a generic shopping template.

Nutrition balance

Protein, carbs, fats and calories are evaluated per meal and across the full week.

Budget control

A weekly spend limit shapes the ranking, so the plan stays within your means.

Cooking time limits

Heavy meals on busy days, quick ones when the schedule fills up. Time is a first-class criterion.

Expiry-aware planning

Ingredients closest to their expiration date are prioritised so nothing goes to waste.

Variety & preferences

Dietary restrictions are respected and meal variety is encouraged across the week.

Explained rankings

Every recommendation comes with a short rationale — the “why” is never a black box.
